An open research exploration

Could the search for energy help explain the rise of intelligence?

Stars pour energy into the universe. Life captures a small part of it, uses it to maintain itself, and eventually releases it again. Could the ability to find and use energy gradients help explain why sensing, prediction, planning and action evolve?

See what the experiment foundTry the simulation
01 / The idea

Life as a way of using energy gradients

The starting point is not that the universe has a goal. It is that energy flows through systems, and some arrangements of matter can make use of those flows.

01 · A source and a sinkA hot star radiates energy into colder surroundings. The difference in temperature makes useful work possible.
02 · A planet intercepts someA planet absorbs part of the incoming energy and eventually sends energy back out, mostly as heat.
03 · Life captures a fractionLiving systems use energy to build, repair and reproduce. They do not keep it forever; energy ultimately flows onward.
04 · Selection shapes strategiesStructures that access usable energy more effectively may survive and reproduce more successfully.
Revised, 23 November 2026: follow-up experiments changed this picture. Most usable energy is locked up by gravity and unfused hydrogen; starlight is one product of unlocking it. Non-living structures also drain gradients and persist, so life is not alone in step 03. What heredity with variation adds is improvement, which should pay most when the environment changes. In this model, carriers of the cognitive traits win on a shared planet while lowering its total throughput, and much of the rise in those traits matches neutral drift. See the paper, version 1.2.

What is entropy?

In thermodynamics, entropy is a measure connected to how widely energy is spread and how much of it remains available to do useful work. It is more precise than the everyday phrase “disorder”.

What is dissipation?

Dissipation is the spreading out or degrading of a concentrated energy difference. For example, friction turns organised motion into heat that is harder to turn back into motion.

Where might agency fit?

Sensing, learning, prediction and movement could help a living system find useful energy. That is a mechanism to test, not a conclusion we build into the definition of life.

The boundary of the claim: the second law says the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease. It does not say that life must appear, that intelligence is inevitable, or that evolution always finds the globally most dissipative arrangement. Those are additional hypotheses, and they need separate evidence.
02 / The test

Remove the advantage by assumption. Keep the mechanisms.

An earlier model gave behavioural sophistication a direct boost to harvesting capacity. That made it difficult to tell whether information and movement could earn their keep on their own. In this experiment, that direct bonus was removed.

72

simulation runs

Two environments × three noise levels × six replicates × two treatments.

220

generations per run

Each run evolves a population of simplified, competing entities on a spatial energy landscape.

6 × 6

matched comparisons

Each environment/noise cell has six pairs: cognitive mechanisms enabled and a control with those traits disabled.

What remained in the model?

CapabilityIts role in the experiment
SensingReduces noise in observations of local energy availability.
Information processingHelps extract a useful signal from noisy observations.
PredictionImproves estimates of near-future conditions.
PlanningControls how much the forecast influences a choice.
ActionAllows an entity to move towards a neighbouring patch it judges more promising.

The harvesting capacity equation now depends on collector area, technology, cooperation and local infrastructure. Sensing, processing, prediction, planning and action do not multiply that capacity directly. They can affect resource access only through observation and movement, while still carrying energetic costs.

We tested two kinds of landscape: strong, predictable hotspots, where resource-rich regions persist in a fairly regular way; and volatile, shifting hotspots, where local conditions change more erratically. Observation and prediction noise were set at 0.1, 0.5 and 1.5 times the spatial standard deviation of the resource field. In plain language, that means low, medium and high uncertainty.

Each cognitive-enabled run was matched to a control with the same starting locations and the same external weather sequence. The control could still evolve collecting capacity, cooperation and technology-like traits; it simply could not evolve the five information/action traits.

03 / What happened

More energy per entity. Less energy in total.

The most consistent result is a trade-off. In all six environment/noise combinations, the cognitive-enabled treatment had higher mean captured power per model entity. But it also supported a smaller population and lower total biological energy throughput.

The local winner was not the collective winner. The entities with information and movement capabilities captured more energy per entity on average, while the less cognitively equipped populations harnessed more energy in total.

How consistent was the per-entity advantage?

Across the 36 matched replicate pairs, the cognitive treatment had higher power per entity in 35 pairs. The average paired advantage ranged from about 6.5 to 16.4 GW per model entity across the six conditions. The calculated 95% confidence intervals for those mean differences were above zero in all six conditions. Six replicates per condition is still a small sample, so these intervals should be treated as exploratory, not as a final estimate of a real-world effect.

What seems to be happening?

The cognitive-enabled populations move more often and occupy fewer patches. They become more concentrated around high-value locations. That can raise the share captured by each remaining entity, but it can also leave large parts of the landscape less occupied and reduce the total population supported by the model. The result is compatible with an exploration-and-concentration effect, but the current experiment does not isolate movement from information processing.

Important distinction: biological energy capture is not the same thing as planetary entropy production. The current model’s planetary radiative-entropy measure also depends on an assumed life-driven albedo change. We do not interpret these power results as proof that intelligence increases planetary entropy production.
04 / What this does and does not say

A useful result is not necessarily a confirmation.

Supported by this experiment

After removing the direct harvesting bonus, the combined information-and-movement traits still evolved to non-zero levels. The package was associated with higher mean captured power per model entity, under these model rules.

Not established

We have not shown that information processing alone caused the advantage, that intelligence is inevitable, or that evolution maximizes the total entropy production of a planet.

The next decisive test

Separate information from movement: compare information processing with movement held fixed, movement with information processing held fixed, and both together. Include random movement and no-movement controls.

That factorial experiment is the next step. It will tell us whether the apparent advantage comes from better information, from moving around, or from the combination of the two.
